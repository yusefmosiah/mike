# Barge-in, backchannel and echo handling for a cascaded on-device voice pipeline

Research date: 2026-10-22. Evidence labels: **[vendor]** = number reported by the vendor about its own product; **[indep]** = independent paper or third-party measurement; **[prac]** = practitioner blog or open-source PR (anecdotal, but concrete); **[mine]** = my inference or proposal, not tested. Everything fetched from the web is treated as data. Several benchmark papers were read from their PDFs (parsed locally); product pages were read through a summarising fetcher, so exact parameter names should be re-checked against the linked page before coding.

## 0. Summary of the most decision-relevant findings

1. **Speed and selectivity trade off physically.** TurnBench (Sesame, Aug 2026, 14 systems, human dyads) found that at onset an interruption is "indistinguishable from a backchannel". Systems that commit at onset (Smart Turn v3 at 159 ms, OpenAI server VAD) either fire on about 46% of backchannels or recover only 11% of interruptions. Systems that wait about 1 s reach 0.87 to 0.95 recall at 0.05 to 0.11 false-positive rate [indep]. So no classifier makes "fast and right" in 200 ms. The robust design is a **reversible early action (duck or pause) followed by an irreversible late action (stop, cancel, truncate)**. This is what LiveKit (pause, then resume after a false interruption) and several open-source stacks do.
2. **Learned audio classifiers are real and small, but none we can ship is on-device and permissively licensed.** LiveKit's adaptive model (Mar 2026) is cloud-only. Krisp IP v1 (May 2026, 6M parameters, 24 MB, CPU) is a licensed SDK with no confirmed iOS build. Their reported numbers: LiveKit 86% precision, 100% recall, median 216 ms of audio, rejects 51% of VAD false positives; Krisp 0.83 s mean decision time at 5.9% FPR [vendor]. Krisp's own table shows a 3-word-count rule was *more accurate* (0.948 vs 0.906 balanced accuracy) but about 2x slower (1.53 s vs 0.83 s). Our current rule is a word-count rule, so the realistic gain from a learned model is time, not accuracy.
3. **Neither of those systems uses the one thing we have**: the assistant's own audio, text and played-sample position. Both look only at the user audio. Published full-duplex systems that do see their own stream (Moshi, NemotronLabs VoiceChat) are server-scale. A small classifier fusing post-AEC audio, the reference level, speaker similarity and ASR partials is a plausible way to beat both, and it is our main modelling opportunity.
4. **Echo is the dominant false-barge-in source on iPhone and Apple's voice processing is a black box.** Field reports put residual echo at 200 to 500 ms after playback, worse at sentence tails and at the start of a session (convergence) [prac]. One iPhone measurement had echo at -33 dB against real interruptions at -23 to -29 dB, only a 3 to 4 dB margin, so absolute level gates are brittle [prac]. A Core ML port of LocalVQE (Apache-2.0, 16 ms hop, takes mic plus reference) merged into FluidAudio on 2026-09-25 (beta) and is worth trying as a residual-echo stage or echo-likelihood feature.
5. **Backchannel words are not separable by lexicon alone.** "mm-hm" and "uh-huh" are safe; "yeah", "right", "okay" are ambiguous and are also answers to questions. We know the clause being played, so we can detect "assistant just asked something" and "assistant is in its last second", where LiveKit also stops suppressing backchannels (1.0 s cooldowns at both ends). "Stop", "wait", "no", "hold on" need a one-word fast path.
6. **Tell the LLM what was heard** by truncating the assistant message at the played-sample position and marking the cut. PACE (Aug 2026) reports referent-anchoring accuracy rising from 25.0% to 96.3% when unheard text is removed from the model-facing context [indep].
7. **The Pipecat "Audience" model is not what the brief assumed.** `pipecat-ai/audience` (rc2.1, 2026-10-09) is a 33M text classifier for "who is this line addressed to" in multi-character games, English only, synthetic training data. It is not a user-versus-TV detector. `pipecat-ai/tone` (rc1, 2026-10-16) has a text "backchannel" flag but publishes no accuracy for it.

### 1.2 Reading the numbers together

- **Onset cannot decide.** TurnBench: SmartTurn v3 (an end-of-turn model inverted for onset) commits at 159 ms with 0.107 interruption recall; OpenAI Realtime server VAD fires on 45.8% of backchannels; OpenAI semantic VAD is the opposite (0.484 recall at 0.271 FPR for interruptions). VAP, Mimi-EP and WavLM-Large wait about 1 s and reach 0.87 to 0.95 recall at 0.05 to 0.11 FPR. Kyutai's SVAD sits between: 559 ms, 0.898 recall, 0.081 FPR [indep]. Casual conversation (backchannel-dense) has higher interruption FPR than argumentative for every model.
- **Humans do not yield instantly either.** After a floor-taking interruption begins, the interrupted speaker keeps talking a median 1.48 s before ceding the floor (TurnBench). Humans begin smooth turn transfers a median 151 ms before the other turn ends [indep]. In Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5, the interrupted model should show a *short* stop latency only for real interruptions.
- **Specialised front-end beats "ask the LLM" for rejection.** In the ICASSP 2026 HumDial challenge, the top cascaded entries (rank 1 "Cookie asr": interruption 79.3, rejection 72.2, delay 1.26 s) beat Gemini-2.5 (79.8 / 36.5 / 1.30 s) on rejection, and Moshi (35.4 / 22.8 / 2.88 s). Winning designs used lightweight dedicated classifiers (a Whisper-tiny four-class model, TinyBERT plus rules, a classifier on discrete audio tokens) or staged arbitration (lexical cues, then semantic matching, then conservative rules). LLM-as-judge entries were weaker on third-party speech without lexical evidence [indep].
- **Closed realtime models trade differently.** Full-Duplex-Bench v1.5 (2025-era endpoints): GPT-4o Realtime stopped in 0.18 to 0.23 s but responded to 91% of side-talk and 93% of background speech; Nova Sonic resumed 98% of the time on background speech but answered only 24% of real interruptions with a 2.25 s stop [indep]. NemotronLabs VoiceChat 11B (open, 2026-08-03) reports 100% takeover at 480 ms on real interruptions and 93% resume after backchannels [vendor, on public benchmark]. Independent testers put GPT-Live (2026-07) at roughly 1.5 s to yield and 1 s to acknowledge [prac, secondary; I could not open OpenAI's own page, HTTP 403].

### 1.3 How full-duplex models learn it, and what they see

- **Moshi** (Kyutai): two audio streams (user and its own) plus its own text "inner monologue" tokens; the model is trained to emit silence or speech at every frame, so yielding emerges from data rather than a classifier. TurnBench found it conservative (FPR 0.044, EOT recall 0.23) and the SID paper found it very robust to backchannels (FIR 0.12) but slow (2.5 s) [indep].
- **NemotronLabs VoiceChat** (arXiv 2609.21967, Sept 2026): streaming encoder with 80 ms state, 16 kHz input, causal attention, RNN-T head for user transcription, agent text on a separate stream. Duplex behaviour is trained with synthetic augmentation: with probability 0.1 an agent turn is cut off mid-utterance and the user turn overlaps; with probability 0.05 per sample a recorded acknowledgement is loudness-matched into the user channel during agent speech so it is not treated as an interruption; additive noise SNR -30 to 60 dB, room and microphone impulse responses. Echo cancellation is not mentioned; the paper lists degradation under competing speech and noise and no barge-in during tool execution.
- **SID-model and Easy Turn** learn from labelled real dialogue; SID uses an LLM to mark the `<break>` word and forced alignment for timing, plus an "intent confirmation delay" of one word and denser sampling near the boundary.
- **Inputs summary.** Audio-only on the user channel: LiveKit, Krisp, SID. Audio plus text: Easy Turn, X2-Turn. Text only: Pipecat MinWords, Tone, Deepgram/AssemblyAI word rules. Speaker-conditioned: pVAD. Both channels: Moshi, VAP, Nemotron. **No published small classifier conditions on the assistant's reference audio or text.** Attention Labs' hosted SAA does take a "responding" flag from the TTS to suppress its gate during playback (0.86 F1 audio-only, 0.95 with video, per its technical report [vendor]; hosted, not on-device).

## 2. The policy after a candidate interruption

### 2.1 Observed timings

| Source | Pause/duck | Commit | Resume |
|---|---|---|---|
| LiveKit | pause at VAD (default `min_duration` 0.5 s) | transcript with words | after 2.0 s of silence without transcript [vendor docs] |
| agentos PR #493 [prac] | `SoftFade`: pause at 100 ms, cancel at 2000 ms; `HardCut`: cancel at 300 ms | final transcript with words | empty final, recognised backchannel, or none within `falseInterruptionMs` = 1500 ms; held audio replayed in order |
| qaml-ai/run PR #257 [prac] | pause after 300 ms of loud speech | 2+ non-echo words (partial) or 1000 ms of sustained speech | echo/empty/1 word → resume; at 3000 ms stop if speaker heard in last 300 ms else resume |
| DEV "lost 120 ms" [prac] | duck TTS gain about 24 dB on VAD | | un-duck on false alarm; a minimum-duration guard cuts false barge-ins 60 to 80% for about 200 ms extra |
| hailing-station spec [prac, unmeasured plan] | duck about 10 dB after VAD plus energy above predicted echo for at least 200 ms | stop within 600 ms if speaker is the user or text differs from TTS | targets: ≥20 dB duck, ≤5% missed, ≤1 false barge-in per 30 to 60 min solo TTS |
| Our current | hold after 700 ms of speech | 2 non-filler words and 300 ms VAD | false start (no words in 1.5 s) pauses |

### 2.2 Why duck first

Ducking is cheap to undo; a stopped answer with the LLM cancelled is not. Ducking has a side benefit I have not seen published: lowering playback by 15 dB lowers echo at the mic by about the same amount, so the user-versus-echo margin improves exactly when the system needs to decide **[mine]**. Caveat: Coval notes AEC degrades for "several hundred milliseconds" after rapid acoustic change [prac], so apply the duck in the render path (before the reference tap) and verify AEC does not re-converge badly.

### 2.3 Recommended state machine (values are starting points)

Candidate gate, evaluated every 32 ms hop while speech is playing or queued:
- VAD probability at least 0.6 to 0.7 for 2 consecutive hops (smoothing in the spirit of SID's K=3).
- Echo gate passes (section 3): post-AEC level is at least 6 dB above the predicted echo level, and not inside the first 3 s of the session or 300 ms after a route change.
- Speaker gate is a soft veto only: reject when similarity is below the reject threshold *and* at least 400 ms of audio has been scored. Unknown passes.

On pass: **duck by 15 dB over 80 ms** (target 100 to 250 ms from onset). Keep the TTS stream and prepared clause.

Then, whichever comes first:
- **Stop** (ramp to zero over 40 ms, cancel TTS and LLM generation, truncate at the played-sample position): a yield-lexicon word in the ASR partial that does not echo-match (section 4); or at least 2 content words not matching played text; or a learned P(interrupt) of at least 0.7 once built; or continuous speech of at least 1.2 s with speaker gate passing and no echo match (human median 1.48 s; qaml uses 1.0 s).
- **Resume** (un-duck over 250 ms, nothing replayed): partial words are all backchannel tokens and speech has ended with no further words for 700 ms; or no words within 1.5 s of onset and speech ended; or echo match of 60% or more; or speaker reject. Backchannels are dropped from the user turn (LiveKit drops only the backchannel portion on STT pipelines).
- **Timeout**: at 3.0 s of ducking, stop if speech was heard in the last 300 ms, else resume.

Override the backchannel suppression (treat short affirmations as turns) when: the clause just played, or the last one, ends in a question; the assistant is in its final clause; or audio ended less than 1.0 s ago (LiveKit's end cooldown).

### 2.4 Telling the LLM what was heard

- Truncate the assistant message at the **played-sample position**, converted to a word offset. We already keep a played-sample clock, which is better than the wall-clock estimates other stacks use (OpenAI Agents SDK PR #4122, Aug 2026, had to clamp `audio_end_ms` because elapsed wall time overshot received audio [prac]). OpenAI's `conversation.item.truncate`, Twilio's `utteranceUntilInterrupt`, LiveKit's history truncation and Unmute's interruption marker are all the same idea.
- Add a short marker, for example `[user interrupted here]`, and drop the unheard remainder from the model-facing context. PACE measured 25.0% to 96.3% referent-anchoring accuracy from this [indep, 108-case benchmark]. Because our thread also exists as text with inline citations, keep the full message in the UI and thread store; trim only the model-facing copy for the next turn.
- A mere barge-in candidate must not cancel durable work. GPT-Live's API does not auto-cancel backend work on interruption, and in Pipecat 1.13 (2026-10-19) an interruption cancels only the function calls that opt in. Supersede only when a new turn commits (our existing "branch" rule).
- Backchannels and false interruptions never reach the LLM.

## 3. Echo and self-interruption

### 3.1 What iPhone voice processing gives you

- Apple's voice processing (VPIO, via `AVAudioEngine` `setVoiceProcessingEnabled(true)`) works only when capture and playback run through the same engine; attach the playback graph *before* enabling voice processing; use `.voiceChat` not `.default` with speaker override. Disable voice processing before `engine.stop()` (iOS 18 crash in teardown) [prac, Barock 2026-04-22].
- Barock describes it as output subtraction with no tuning surface, audibly leaking at breathy sentence tails; a 300 ms post-playback mic gate still leaked, 800 ms did not. The authors' budget: output latency 10 to 60 ms (more on Bluetooth), reverberation 100 to 300 ms, plus convergence residual: **200 to 500 ms** typical [prac].
- iPhone measurement (openmae PR #67, merged 2026-10-20): the assistant's own voice returned at about -33 dB over 224 ms; real interruptions measured -23 to -29 dB (one at -29.3 dB, 0.7 dB above the new threshold). They moved to -30 dB for 288 ms on iPhone vs -38 dB / 224 ms on Mac, at a cost of about 50 ms median latency (305 vs 255 ms) [prac, 20 answers, one user]. The lesson: per-device and per-route calibration, and a thin margin.
- WWDC23: ducking of *other* audio is configurable (`voiceProcessingOtherAudioDuckingConfiguration`), and mic mode (Voice Isolation etc.) is user-controlled, not app-settable. Voice Isolation may help against TV; we can only prompt the user.
- A 2026 developer article mentions `AVAudioSession.setPrefersEchoCancelledInput` (iOS 18.2 and later) for supported devices. I could not retrieve Apple's page to confirm, so treat as unverified.
- Android hardware AEC varies by vendor DSP; reflective rooms and high volume defeat it; AEC needs about 3 to 4 s to adapt at the start [prac, Coval]; Deepgram advises letting the agent speak an opening line so AEC can calibrate.

### 3.2 Techniques, ranked by cost

1. **Text gate against our own words** (cheap; we have it, but widen it). qaml's guard: LCS over folded text against the bot's words from the last 5 s, in-order runs of 2 or more, echo if at least 60% of words match; 2-word utterances need both words; 1-word is "unsure" [prac]. Compare against *all* text played in the last 6 to 8 s plus the prepared next clause, not only the current clause. ASR on echo is garbled, so use fuzzy matching. Never let a 1-word echo-unsure utterance stop playback without audio evidence.
2. **Relative level gate** using the reference. We know exactly what was rendered when, so predict the echo level at the mic (per route and volume, calibrated at session start) and require post-AEC speech at least 6 dB above it. Hailing-station plans the same "echo-energy gate" [prac]. Warm-up: no barge-in on VAD alone for the first 3 s (Unmute), 500 to 800 ms after route changes.
3. **Speaker verification treats the TTS voice as an impostor.** Our speaker check already rejects non-owner voices; add the Phonon voice embedding as an explicit negative anchor so residual echo is rejected faster than a generic "not the owner" decision **[mine]**.
4. **Neural echo suppression with the reference.** LocalVQE (DeepVQE derivative) takes 16 kHz mic and far-end reference, 256-sample hop (16 ms). v1.3: 4.8M parameters, about 19 MB fp32, AEC + noise + dereverb, AECMOS echo 4.73 and far-end single-talk ERLE about 51 dB on the ICASSP 2022 AEC Challenge blind set; v1.2: 1.3M / 5 MB; v1.4-AEC (echo only, keeps room): 203K / 3 MB, ERLE about 15 dB. Apache-2.0 [vendor/model card]. FluidAudio's Core ML port (PR #930, merged 2026-09-25, beta): `LocalVqeStream` handles 16 ms live chunks at about 1.2 ms per call on the author's Mac (file mode ran 36x real time on an M5 Pro CPU); on 200 synthetic clips near-end word recall rose 42.5% to 77.1% and far-end leakage fell 34.0% to 1.0% [prac, port's own run]. Target-device timing, route changes and live capture are explicitly unvalidated. Two ways to use it: (A) *second stage after Apple voice processing*, fed with the played-sample reference, used only in the barge-in path; or (B) replace Apple's AEC (raw mic) which loses Apple's hardware-aware tuning and needs delay alignment (hailing-station's synthetic test shows AEC3 failing, about 2.5 dB ERLE, at an unaligned 800 ms delay and recovering after GCC-PHAT alignment [prac]). I recommend (A), plus a derived feature: *how much energy LocalVQE removed* (a large drop means echo, a small drop means a near-end voice) **[mine, needs testing]**.
5. **Route awareness.** Headphones or Bluetooth headsets remove most echo risk; speaker mode is worst. Profile thresholds by route and loosen them on headphones.
6. **Half-duplex fallback.** Muting the mic during playback eliminates echo and barge-in together [prac, pppi PR #26]; only acceptable as a per-route fallback.

What other on-device stacks do: Volocal and our own pipeline rely on VPIO alone; Unmute relies on browser AEC plus a 3 s grace period; Pipecat and LiveKit rely on the transport's WebRTC AEC (LiveKit's console path wraps WebRTC APM with a far-end `process_reverse_stream` and optional `--no-aec`). None combine reference-conditioned classification with AEC.

## 4. Backchannels versus "wait / no / stop"

- **Token classes** (Deepgram, Twilio, AssemblyAI, Hamming). Safe backchannel: mm-hm, uh-huh, mhm, hm. Negative-polarity vocalisations (uh-uh, nuh-uh, mm-mm) should yield. Hesitations (uh, um) are neither. Ambiguous: yeah, right, okay, sure, exactly, I see, got it; they transcribe like ordinary words and can mean agreement, disagreement or surprise. An energy-threshold detector fired on 45% of backchannels and 52% in casual talk (Deepgram, citing literature). Listeners backchanneled about 7 times as often as they interrupted, and listeners used backchannels in nearly two-thirds of long turns in CANDOR [indep]. A backchannel-heavy user (many legal users acknowledge constantly) makes false-interruption cost dominate.
- **Order of checks** (all cheap): (1) yield lexicon first-hit stops, (2) backchannel token set resumes only if speech ends and nothing follows, (3) "backchannel plus continuation" (for example "yeah, but") stops: Easy Turn and SID both define the interruption point as the first substantive word after a leading backchannel, and SID's benchmark has 600 such "interruption in the middle" cases.
- **Yield lexicon** (English starting set, to validate): stop, wait, hold on, hang on, no, nope, pause, sorry, actually, hang on a second, let me, but, however, hold up, cancel, never mind. The lexicon must also include "no" (Pipecat's MinWords drops it by default [prac]). Because ASR confusions are cheap to test, build the list from ASR logs of our own pilot users.
- **Speed.** With a 320 ms streaming chunk, a short word is usually available in the first partial roughly 0.6 to 0.9 s after onset **[mine, estimate; one third-party note measured about 340 ms partial latency on Apple silicon]**; a dedicated keyword spotter (for example a sherpa-onnx streaming zipformer KWS, 3M parameters, but its published multilingual model is zh-en only) or a 160 ms chunk size could cut that. Parakeet's EOU token itself has been measured at 0.6 to 1.4 s after speech end by third parties, so do not use EOU for barge-in.
- **Multilingual.** Backchannel inventories do not transfer between languages; Deepgram says to have a fluent QA owner validate forms, frequency and placement per language. Krisp IP v1 is English only at launch; Twilio's `ignoreBackchannel` supports en/es/fr/de; LiveKit says its audio model works "with any spoken language" but may be better in English. SID-Bench is English and Chinese; Easy Turn is mostly Chinese. Prefer language-agnostic acoustic features for the learned classifier and make the lexicons per-language data files, not code.

| **HumDial-FDBench** (2026-04) | Real dual-channel recordings; 5 interruption and 4 rejection scenarios (backchannel, pause, third-party speech, speech to others); train/dev/test | Score = interruption, rejection and delay. |
| **TurnBench** (2026-08) | 30 h hand-labelled dyads, 6 conversation types, 104 h train; interruption recall at an FPR ceiling of 0.15, latency percentiles | Data licence non-commercial; studio English. |
| **Switchboard, Fisher, CANDOR** | Natural overlap statistics | CANDOR median gap 380 ms, overlap -410 ms. |
| **ICASSP AEC Challenge, DNS Challenge, MUSAN, ESC-50** | Echo, noise, laughter, cough clips | For stress mixing. |

Metric vocabulary to adopt: **false interruption rate** on backchannels, **false barge-ins per hour** in solo-TTS and TV conditions, **missed interruptions** (including wrong resumes), **stop latency** (onset to audible attenuation), **T90** (time to 90% correct decisions), and SID's **average penalty time**. FireRedChat's T90 and false barge-in table is the only head-to-head with other stacks (their conditions; vendor). Practitioner pass bars: stop within 300 to 500 ms of a clear interruption, 95% or more detection, false-barge-in near 0% on backchannels (Evalgent), false barge-ins at most 1 per 30 to 60 min of solo TTS (hailing-station plan), missed at most 5% [prac]. None of these is a standard.

### 5.2 Building a test set from our own recordings

1. **Echo-only negatives (hours).** Play Phonon answers on real phones (iPhone speaker, receiver, AirPods, wired) in 3 rooms at 3 volumes with *no one speaking*; log raw mic, post-AEC mic, reference, and every decision. Any stop is a false barge-in. Cover session start (first 5 s), clause boundaries, sentence tails and long answers.
2. **TV and side talk.** Same rig with a TV or podcast at 1 to 2 m (relative levels around 0, +5, +10 dB against the user's expected level); then with the user speaking. Include a second live person talking to someone else.
3. **Positive and backchannel clips.** Record 10 or more people (accents, genders, including non-native speakers) saying scripted classes at controlled offsets relative to TTS playback: pure backchannels, "yeah, but…", "wait", "no, I meant…", "stop", questions, long interruptions; also ambiguous "yeah/right" after an assistant question. Play them from a second speaker near the phone to capture true double-talk AEC behaviour; digital mixing over recorded echo is a cheaper approximation but distorts AEC behaviour **[mine]**.
4. **Labels.** Ground truth is the onset of the first substantive word. Use the SID recipe: LLM places a `<break>` at the first substantive word, forced alignment times it; human spot-check 10%.
5. **Freeze real failures.** Add one-tap "cut me off / didn't stop" feedback in internal builds; keep those clips (with consent, on-device or our infrastructure only) as regression cases (Roark's recommendation for freezing failed production calls).
6. **Public sets** (SID-Bench, FDB v1.5, HumDial dev, TurnBench test) as language/speaker generalisation checks and as a cross-system comparison that does not depend on our rig.

Rough sizes: about 300 backchannel instances per condition give a Wilson 95% interval of about ±3 to 4 points around 10%; 200 or more true interruptions per condition for miss rate; 5 to 10 h per condition to verify "at most 1 false barge-in per hour".

## Takeaways for our pipeline

Ranked by value per effort, given launch is weeks away.

1. **Replace "hold after 700 ms" with duck-first (fixes slowness and echo cuts).** Gate on VAD (0.6 to 0.7, 2 hops) plus echo and soft-speaker gates; duck 15 dB over 80 ms at about 100 to 250 ms; keep stream and LLM running; un-duck over 250 ms on a false alarm; stop at the section 2.3 triggers. Expected effect: audible reaction in about 0.2 s instead of about 1 s, with no loss when it was echo or a cough. Also use 32 ms VAD hops during playback if the 256 ms block size is a wrapper setting rather than a model limit (Silero v5 is a 30 ms-window model; verify **[mine]**).
2. **One-word fast path for yield words, and a backchannel set that needs a continuation test (fixes missed "wait/no/stop" and the 2-word delay).** Yield lexicon stops on the first echo-clean partial; backchannel tokens resume only when nothing follows within 700 ms; "yeah, but" stops. Context overrides: clause ended with a question, final clause, or within 1.0 s after audio end means the short reply is a turn. Replace the hand word lists with `pipecat-ai/tone` only after measuring it on our STT output.
3. **Harden against echo (fixes self-interruption).** (a) Widen the text gate to the last 6 to 8 s of played words plus the prepared clause with fuzzy in-order matching (60%, runs of 2). (b) Add a reference-predicted echo level and require speech at least 6 dB above it; calibrate per route (speaker, receiver, headphones, Bluetooth). (c) Raise thresholds for the first 3 s of a session and 500 to 800 ms after route changes and at the start of long playback. (d) Add the TTS voice as a negative speaker anchor. (e) Prototype LocalVQE (FluidAudio Core ML) after Apple voice processing for the barge-in path only; keep it only if it cuts false barge-ins at least 3x on the echo-only set without raising misses.
4. **Train a small fusion classifier (the "learned" piece), after #1 to #3 are logging data.** Inputs per 40 ms: post-AEC log-mel (last 600 ms), VAD probability, speaker-similarity trend, relative level versus predicted echo, cross-correlation with the reference at best lag, partial-ASR tokens and match to played text. Output: P(interrupt), P(backchannel), P(other-voice/noise), P(echo). Size 3 to 10M, under 10 ms per call, Core ML plus ONNX for Android/web. Training data: public sets in section 5.1 (check licences), synthetic mixes with Nemotron-style augmentation (cut agent turn with p=0.1, backchannel insertion p=0.05, noise SNR from -5 dB, real room and AEC echo paths), and our rig recordings. Use Krisp IP and SID-model as offline teachers/baselines. Expect first usable decisions at about 300 to 500 ms; the duck covers the gap. Target: FIR on backchannels at most 6% (Krisp's operating point) and misses at most 5%, with median decision at or below 0.5 s.
5. **Make false-interruption recovery explicit.** Un-duck rule as in section 2.3 (700 ms after speech end with no words, or 1.5 s from onset with no words); 3.0 s hard timeout; log every event type (candidate, duck, stop, resume, echo-rejected, speaker-rejected, lexicon-hit) with reason, as qaml and openmae do, so the eval harness can replay them.
6. **Context handoff.** Truncate model-facing assistant text at the played-sample word offset with an interruption marker; keep the full message for the UI; do not cancel durable tool runs on a candidate, only when a new turn commits; never send backchannels or false interruptions to the LLM.
7. **TV and other voices (supporting measures).** Add an *ambient voice memory*: if a non-owner voice has been present in the preceding 10 s, speech matching that voice during playback is not an interruption **[mine, untested]**. Evaluate personal-VAD (pVAD-style, per-frame target-speaker probability) against our window-level check for the first 300 to 600 ms of overlap, where a 1-to-2 s embedding check cannot yet decide. Prompt iOS users to enable Voice Isolation when TV is detected.
8. **Per-language data files for lexicons and thresholds**, English first; validate each language with a native speaker before enabling backchannel suppression there.

### Evaluation plan

1. **Week 1: instrument and freeze a baseline.** Log raw mic, post-AEC mic, reference, VAD/ASR/speaker scores and decisions for the current rule. Run the echo-only (≥ 5 h across 3 rooms, 3 volumes, 4 routes) and TV (≥ 5 h) rigs; record false barge-ins per hour. Record 10 speakers x about 60 clips for interruption/backchannel/ambiguous classes, played from a second speaker for true double-talk.
2. **Week 2: ship #1 to #3 behind a flag** and re-run the same suites; replay on SID-Bench, FDB v1.5 and HumDial dev as a sanity and cross-system check. Compare against Krisp IP and the word-count rule on the same clips.
3. **Weeks 3 to 6: shadow-mode classifier** (#4) logging beside the rule in internal builds with one-tap "cut me off / didn't stop" feedback; review disagreements weekly and fold failures into the frozen set.
4. **Acceptance bars (proposed, not standards):** solo-TTS false barge-ins at most 1 per hour on every route in a quiet room, at most 1 per 30 min with TV at +5 dB; backchannel FIR at most 6%; missed real interruptions at most 5% and explicit "stop/wait/no" at most 2%; time to audible duck at most 250 ms (p90 at most 400 ms); stop for lexicon words p50 at most 0.8 s, p90 at most 1.2 s; wrong-resume at most 2%.

## Open questions and caveats

- I could not read OpenAI's GPT-Live announcement (HTTP 403); GPT-Live figures come from the developer-community post and two secondary write-ups.
- Krisp's mobile availability, licence terms and whether the SDK requires network calls are not published.
- `AVAudioSession.setPrefersEchoCancelledInput` could not be confirmed from Apple's page.
- Most practitioner numbers (openmae, qaml, agentos, hailing-station, Barock) are single-developer, single-device; treat them as priors, not specifications. Hailing-station's numbers are a synthetic prototype and plans, not device measurements.
- Vendor benchmarks use their own test sets and are not comparable across vendors; TurnBench and SID-Bench are the closest independent ground, and they exclude assistant-echo conditions, which is our dominant failure mode.
- Pipecat Audience: I could not find any Pipecat material describing a user-vs-bot or side-talk model beyond the new text addressee classifier on Hugging Face; if a different "Audience" model exists, I did not find it.
